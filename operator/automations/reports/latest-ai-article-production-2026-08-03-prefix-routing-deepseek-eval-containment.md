## Deep evidence

Completed `operator/diy-project-blogs/projects/prefix-affinity-cache-routing-audit` with:

- 200 matched repeats per scenario, four scenarios, and four routing policies;
- 3,200 repeat-policy rows and 5,000 paired bootstrap resamples;
- random and least-loaded baselines;
- uniform-prefix and cache-disabled controls;
- strict prefix affinity and a preregistered 25 ms bounded-affinity ablation;
- locked configuration, simulator, research log, run summary, repeat CSV, aggregate JSON, claim table, result figure, SHA-256 ledger, and version-1 evidence manifest.

In the focal 45%-hot, 120-rps cell, least-loaded routing achieved a 58.56% median hit rate and 78.97 ms median p99. Strict affinity raised hit rate to 99.67% but raised median p99 to 4,378.38 ms and failed the 250 ms SLO in every repeat. Bounded affinity achieved 97.58% hits and 54.14 ms median p99, passing every repeat; its paired p99 improvement over least loaded was 24.77 ms, 95% interval [24.50, 25.04].

The controls narrowed the claim. Strict affinity improved uniform-load p99 by 38.91 ms, so affinity is not intrinsically harmful. With caching disabled, bounded affinity added 7.43 ms, interval [7.17, 7.67], with zero hit gain. The article therefore recommends an explicit measured exchange between queue delay and hit value, not a universal 25 ms threshold or production-GPU speedup claim.
