## Deep evidence project

Created `operator/diy-project-blogs/projects/hedged-request-load-audit` with a
version-1 evidence manifest, fixed configuration, dependency-free runner,
figure renderer, README, 5,120 repeat-policy rows, aggregate results,
statistical analysis, focal output capture, and the generated SVG figure.

Design:

- 320 matched repeats of 1,600 requests across four scenarios and four policies.
- 8,192,000 logical policy-request observations.
- no hedge, fixed 250 ms, fixed 500 ms, and queue-capped 250 ms policies.
- moderate independent, near-capacity independent, near-capacity correlated,
  and no-tail control scenarios.
- scenario-specific arrival rates locked after an exploratory screen revealed
  that a common rate made baseline offered load incomparable.
- 5,000 paired bootstrap resamples over repeat-level metrics.

Headline findings:

- In moderate independent tails, fixed 250 ms hedging reduced median p95 from
  1.464 s to 0.422 s and mean completed work from 0.320 s to 0.244 s.
- In near-capacity correlated tails, fixed 250 ms reduced median p95 from 4.883
  s to 3.695 s but raised median maximum queue depth from 9.1 to 17.8.
- Queue-capping in that correlated cell limited median maximum queue depth to
  8.8 but left median p95 at 4.539 s.
- In the no-tail control, fixed 250 ms hedging increased median p95 from 0.690
  s to 1.721 s, mean completed work by 0.0254 worker-seconds, and maximum queue
  depth by 11.45 requests in the paired estimate.

The preregistered strong hypothesis that correlated near-capacity service would
reverse the latency gain was falsified. The public claim was narrowed to a
measured tail-diversity and load-control boundary. No production provider,
network-placement effect, accelerator, or universal hedge threshold was
claimed.
